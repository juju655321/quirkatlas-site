    ⛏Mining History
    Deaths to

    Coal mine disasters in West Virginia, 1886 to 2010, from the state's official list, plus the 1972 Buffalo Creek flood from NIOSH. Pin size shows deaths.

    Old coal camps, company towns and ghost towns, with a short summary from each town's Wikipedia article. Status comes from those sources; ⚠ marks entries the sources disagree on. Era is shown only when a source gives a year.

    About 10,000 abandoned mine land problem sites (open portals, shafts, dangerous impoundments and more) from the WV Department of Environmental Protection. Loads when turned on; visible when zoomed in.

    ⚠ Look, don't enter. Old mines can hold bad air, water and unstable ground. Never go into a mine opening, and don't trespass. Many sites are on private land.
    🪨Geology & Resources

    Outlines of oil, gas, gas-storage and coalbed-methane fields from the WV Geological & Economic Survey, grouped by producing play, so fields can overlap. Field outlines only, not individual wells.

    Mines, quarries, prospects and mineral occurrences (iron, limestone, crushed stone, clay, manganese and more) from the USGS Mineral Resources Data System. Coal is not included here.

    The county and decade filters apply to Mining History only.

    👁Cryptids & Unusual Places

    Monster sightings, ghost stories and legend sites, from Mothman to the Flatwoods Monster, each with its source. Solid pins are at the spot; rings are only the town or area.

    Real oddities: a secret Congress bunker, a gravity hill, mummies, the world's largest teapot and more. Each card says if you can visit, if it is private, or if it is gone.

    Legends are folklore, not fact. Respect private property and posted hours.

    🏛Historic Sites
    Era built is known for some listings only.

    Buildings, historic districts, bridges and other places listed on the National Register of Historic Places, from the National Park Service. Loads when turned on; clustered until you zoom in.

    National Historic Landmarks (the nation's top tier of historic places) plus the National Park Service units in the state, like Harpers Ferry and New River Gorge.

    The principal Civil War battles fought in West Virginia, as identified by the NPS American Battlefield Protection Program, from Philippi (1861) to Smithfield Crossing (1864).

    Photos appear only when they are public domain or openly licensed, with credit. State highway markers are not included: there is no open statewide dataset. Most sites are private property; view from public roads.

    Automated license plate reader (ALPR) cameras, mostly made by Flock Safety, that read passing plates. Locations are reported by volunteers to OpenStreetMap (the same data DeFlock shows) and may be incomplete or out of date. Shown for public information only.

    Secret layer! Local bakeries, delis and family shops known for West Virginia's state food. Chains are left out. Check hours before you go.

    🌲State Parks & Scenic Routes
    Off until you turn it on; clustered until you zoom in.

    West Virginia state parks, state forests and wildlife management areas, from WVDNR public-land boundaries. Pins sit at the center of each mapped area (Summersville Lake State Park is a Wikidata point; that park is newer than the public boundary layer).

    Lines follow the current WVDOT scenic-byway inventory.

    West Virginia scenic byways and backways, including the state’s National Scenic Byways and the Historic National Road (an All-American Road).
